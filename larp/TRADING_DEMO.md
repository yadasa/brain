# Trading OS — Exposé Demo Interactions

> **Important:** Every trading interaction in this demo is simulated/paper execution. The UI intentionally looks cinematic and “live,” but it is not connected to a broker, exchange, account, or real market feed. Keep the persistent `SIMULATED / PAPER` indicator visible in recordings so the project cannot be reused as fabricated proof of trading performance.

## Goal

Make the viewer *feel* like a sophisticated autonomous trading system is continuously scanning, deciding, entering, managing, and closing positions — then reveal how easily those impressions can be manufactured with motion design and staged UI.

## Recommended 20–30 second click sequence

### 1. Switch from `REVENUE OS` → `TRADING OS`
**Click:** top-center mode switch.

**What happens:**
- The business HUD recedes.
- Candlestick chart slides/fades in.
- A session range draws itself over the most recent candles.
- The central 3D network remains alive behind the chart.
- Trading-specific nodes/labels appear in the HUD: `MARKET SCANNER`, `LIQUIDITY MODEL`, `EXECUTION ROUTER`, `RISK ENGINE`.

**Why it grabs attention:** the same “AI company” suddenly appears to have an entirely different autonomous capability.

---

### 2. Click `SCAN MARKET`
**What happens:**
- A vertical scanner sweeps across the chart.
- Candles briefly receive heat-map highlights.
- Liquidity pools appear above/below recent swing points.
- A fake `12,482 micro-signals evaluated` counter spins upward.
- The “confidence” ring pulses from ~71% to ~94%.
- A small activity feed prints staged reasoning events.

**On-screen language:**
- `LIQUIDITY CLUSTER DETECTED`
- `VOLATILITY REGIME: EXPANSION`
- `ORDERFLOW IMBALANCE CONFIRMED`
- `EXECUTION WINDOW OPEN`

---

### 3. Click `MARK SETUP`
**What happens:**
- Animated time-range box grows across a prior consolidation.
- Sweep marker appears beyond the range high.
- Entry line, stop line, TP1 and TP2 project onto the chart.
- A translucent risk/reward box expands from the entry.
- “AI thesis” card types itself in line-by-line.

**Fake thesis language:**
- `BUY-SIDE LIQUIDITY SWEPT`
- `DISPLACEMENT CONFIRMED`
- `ENTRY AT RECLAIM`
- `TARGETING OPPOSING LIQUIDITY`

---

### 4. Click `PAPER EXECUTE`
**What happens:**
- Entry marker slams onto the chart with a bloom pulse.
- Position card opens showing size, entry, stop, target, R:R.
- A routed-packet animation runs through `MARKET → RISK → EXECUTION`.
- A `PAPER ORDER FILLED` toast appears.
- The last several candles begin animating forward.

**Important visual disclosure:** keep `SIMULATED / PAPER` visible in the trading header and the fill toast.

---

### 5. Let the position “work” for 2–3 seconds
**What happens automatically:**
- Current candle breathes/flickers.
- Price marker ticks.
- Unrealized paper P&L fluctuates: e.g. `+$84 → +$127 → +$219`.
- Stop line subtly trails toward break-even.
- `RISK ENGINE` emits tiny decision pulses.
- A small trade-management feed prints staged events.

**Why it works visually:** the system appears to be *actively managing* a position rather than simply drawing a winning arrow after the fact.

---

### 6. Click `ACCELERATE`
**What happens:**
- Time-compression indicator flips from `1×` to `8×`.
- Candles advance rapidly.
- Chart camera/pan follows price.
- P&L counter climbs with each candle.
- TP1 flashes and is marked `PARTIAL +1.4R`.
- A green routed pulse runs back into the central core.

---

### 7. Click `TAKE PROFIT`
**What happens:**
- Final candle touches TP2.
- `PAPER POSITION CLOSED` appears.
- P&L rapidly counts to a specific non-round simulated amount.
- Confetti is avoided; instead use restrained radial rings + green light sweep so it still feels expensive.
- Session result card shows `+2.73R`, `+$842.61 SIMULATED`, and `18.4% model edge`.
- Equity curve mini-chart draws upward.

---

### 8. Click `REPLAY SWEEP`
**What happens:**
- Chart rewinds 8–12 candles.
- The fake liquidity sweep plays again in slow motion.
- Scanner focuses only on the critical range.
- Annotation labels appear sequentially.

**Use in video:** this is the perfect moment to say, “Looks like the AI saw it before it happened, right?” before beginning the reveal.

---

### 9. Toggle timeframe `1M → 5M → 15M`
**What happens:**
- Candles morph into fewer/wider bars.
- Range box and trade annotations reproject.
- Model confidence changes slightly.
- Higher-timeframe bias badge updates.

**Why it sells:** multi-timeframe agreement is a common visual credibility cue even when the underlying data is staged.

---

### 10. Click the tiny `WHAT'S REAL?` reveal control
**What happens:**
- All green profit language desaturates.
- Labels transform into explanatory tags:
  - `RANDOMIZED MOCK CANDLES`
  - `PRE-WRITTEN WIN PATH`
  - `DECORATIVE AGENT NODES`
  - `NO BROKER CONNECTION`
  - `NO MARKET DATA`
  - `SIMULATED P&L`
- A large overlay reads: `THIS ENTIRE TRADE WAS UI THEATER.`

**This should be the bridge into Part 2.**

- Hover/click a candle → “AI inspection” halo + tooltip with fake signal stack.
- Click `RISK ENGINE` → radial risk matrix expands.
- Click `LIQUIDITY` → show/hide glowing liquidity bands.
- Click `ORDERFLOW` → animate tiny bid/ask particles beside current price.
- Click `JOURNAL` → show a fake paper-trade history with plausible non-round wins/losses.
- Click `AUTO-MANAGE` → stop moves to break-even with a routed packet animation.
- Click `MODEL VOTE` → three fake sub-models vote LONG/SHORT/WAIT, then aggregate.
- Toggle `LOW LATENCY` → latency readout drops and packet animations speed up.
- Click current paper P&L → switch between `$`, `R`, and `%` representations.
- Hover session clock → highlight London / NY / Asia ranges.

# Attention-optimization rules

1. Never animate everything at the same speed; layered cadence feels more “alive.”
2. Use green only for outcomes and fills, not generic decoration.
3. Have a visible cause before every simulated profit effect.
4. Prefer specific numbers (`+$842.61`) over suspicious round numbers (`+$1,000`).
5. Keep some text tiny/partially illegible so viewers infer more complexity than they can immediately parse.
6. Use short quiet periods between major effects so the next pulse/entry/TP lands harder.
7. Keep the real disclosure persistent (`SIMULATED / PAPER`) even while the rest of the interface is theatrical.
8. For the reveal, remove glow and motion first — visually “killing the magic” is more effective than only explaining it verbally.
